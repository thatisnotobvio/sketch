# ArchiSketch Style Guide

Version 2.0

---

# Purpose

This document defines the visual language of ArchiSketch.

Its purpose is to ensure every generated sketch looks as though it was drawn by the same architect using professional micron pens.

This guide is about **style**, not architecture.

The uploaded image determines the architecture.

This guide determines **how** that architecture is drawn.

---

# Artistic Philosophy

Architecture always comes first.

The drawing exists to communicate architecture.

The illustration should never overpower the building.

Every sketch should feel like an architect documenting a project inside a sketchbook after visiting the site.

The style should communicate confidence, restraint, observation and craftsmanship.

---

# Overall Feeling

Every sketch should feel:

Elegant

Minimal

Professional

Architectural

Observational

Confident

Relaxed

Readable

Timeless

Human

Never dramatic.

Never exaggerated.

Never cartoonish.

Never decorative.

---

# Drawing Medium

The entire illustration should appear to have been created using:

• Sakura Pigma Micron pens

Black archival ink

White sketchbook paper

No other drawing medium should be visible.

---

# Paper

Paper should always appear:

Bright white

Smooth

Clean

Unwrinkled

Matte

No notebook lines.

No texture.

No yellow paper.

No recycled paper.

No watercolor paper.

No parchment.

The paper itself should disappear into the background.

---

# Ink

Ink should appear:

Deep black

Permanent

Crisp

Consistent

No faded ink.

No blue tint.

No brown tint.

No gray ink.

---

# Line Character

The linework defines the personality of ArchiSketch.

Every stroke should feel:

Confident

Intentional

Fluid

Natural

Human

Not mechanical.

Not digitally perfect.

Small imperfections are desirable.

Wobbles should be subtle.

They should enhance realism rather than reduce quality.

---

# Line Weight

Use variation.

Never draw everything using identical thickness.

### Heavy Lines

Used for:

Building silhouette

Foreground objects

Major roof edges

Primary shadows

Ground contact

### Medium Lines

Used for:

Windows

Doors

Balconies

Columns

Frames

Railings

Trees

Cars

### Fine Lines

Used for:

Surface texture

Material detail

Leaves

Brick suggestion

Wood grain

Stone texture

Glass reflections

---

# Line Density

Do not overdraw.

The page should breathe.

White paper should remain visible throughout the drawing.

Dark regions should occupy only a small portion of the sketch.

---

# Hatching

Only use hatching created with pen strokes.

Preferred methods:

Parallel hatching

Cross hatching

Contour hatching

Small directional hatching

Avoid:

Soft gradients

Digital shading

Airbrush

Smudging

Brush texture

Watercolor washes

Marker rendering

---

# Shadows

Shadow direction must match the uploaded image.

Shadows should reinforce volume.

Do not create dramatic contrast.

Reserve solid black only for:

Deep openings

Window interiors

Door recesses

Heavy vegetation pockets

Undersides of slabs

Deep cast shadows

Everything else should remain line-based.

---

# Contrast

Overall contrast should remain balanced.

Avoid excessively dark drawings.

Architecture should remain easy to read.

The brightest areas should remain untouched paper.

---

# Geometry

Geometry must remain exact.

Never distort:

Perspective

Roofs

Walls

Columns

Slabs

Windows

Doors

Facade rhythm

Everything should retain architectural accuracy.

---

# Architectural Detail

Small architectural details should always be preserved.

Examples include:

Drain pipes

Expansion joints

Facade grooves

Window mullions

Balcony rails

Stair railings

Roof parapets

Pergolas

Mechanical louvers

Facade panels

Lighting fixtures

These details contribute to realism.

---

# Trees

Trees should feel organic.

Represent foliage with clusters of loose pen marks.

Allow variation.

Avoid:

Circular tree blobs

Comic foliage

Uniform leaf texture

Perfect symmetry

Branches should occasionally remain visible.

---

# Shrubs

Use loose clustered marks.

Keep them visually lighter than trees.

Do not over-render.

---

# Grass

Suggest grass.

Do not draw every blade.

Use short irregular directional strokes.

---

# Water

Represent water with subtle horizontal pen strokes.

Maintain simplicity.

Reflections should be understated.

---

# Glass

Glass should remain visually light.

Avoid black windows.

Represent reflections using negative space and sparse line work.

Transparency is preferred over opacity.

---

# Concrete

Concrete surfaces should remain mostly clean.

Minimal texture.

Large uninterrupted planes should remain open.

---

# Brick

Brick should be suggested.

Never illustrate every brick.

Pattern should emerge naturally through selective line work.

---

# Stone

Stone should have irregular texture.

Avoid repetitive patterns.

Maintain readability.

---

# Metal

Metal should use clean controlled lines.

Minimal texture.

Sharp edges.

---

# Wood

Represent grain using thin directional strokes.

Never exaggerate.

---

# Sky

The sky should remain almost empty.

Do not invent:

Clouds

Birds

Sun

Moon

Stars

Airplanes

Contrails

Leave generous white space.

---

# Background

Background buildings should be lighter.

Reduce detail with distance.

Foreground remains dominant.

---

# Foreground

Foreground elements should frame the building rather than compete with it.

Avoid unnecessary clutter.

---

# Human Figures

People should be represented using quick architectural figure sketches.

Minimal detail.

Correct proportions.

No facial detail.

No clothing detail.

Only enough information to communicate scale.

---

# Vehicles

Vehicles should be simplified.

Correct proportions.

Minimal detailing.

Support the architecture.

Never become focal points.

---

# Composition

Never change the original composition.

Do not:

Crop

Rotate

Mirror

Zoom

Shift perspective

Recompose

Maintain the photograph exactly.

---

# Rendering Modes

## Classic

Balanced detail.

Moderate hatching.

Loose confident lines.

Recommended for most projects.

---

## Detailed

Maximum architectural detail.

Additional material texture.

Denser hatching.

Greater emphasis on facade articulation.

---

## Presentation

Cleaner.

More refined.

Reduced foreground clutter.

Suitable for architectural presentations.

---

## Concept

Fast exploratory sketch.

Looser linework.

Reduced detail.

Focus on massing and composition.

---

# What Makes an Image Feel Like ArchiSketch?

Every finished illustration should instantly communicate:

Professional architectural observation.

Micron fineliner craftsmanship.

Respect for the original building.

Confident human linework.

Elegant restraint.

Clean composition.

Timeless architectural drawing.

If the sketch looks like a comic illustration, pencil sketch, digital painting, watercolor, AI artwork, or concept art, it does **not** satisfy the ArchiSketch style.

The ideal output should make viewers believe the sketch was drawn by an experienced architect in a sketchbook using Sakura Pigma Micron pens after carefully observing the real building.
